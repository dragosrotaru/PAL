# Marketplace, access, and society

## Access to the web

As of May 25, 2020, there are ~7.8 billion people in the world:
- ~773 million don't know how to read or write
- ~6.5 billion don't speak English
- ~3.6 billion don't have access to the internet

Take a moment to think about how fortunate we are to meet all the requirements to participate. This is a fundamental part of the human story and most people cannot access it.

## The marketplace of ideas

Should the hypergraph be attached to a mechanism of financial transaction, or left free? Content is created by agents. Agents can be paid for their content through donations. If the creator wishes to keep content paid-only, they should have the right to do so. Should the system specifically enable this? Maybe, for user adoption.

If a company wants to implement DRM, they should be able to. It shouldn't be endorsed. What about copyright?

## Comments as hyperedges

(From a conversation with Jesse)

**Content vs comment dichotomy** — the new web should have no hierarchy between content and comment. All comments are content. Comments should be able to connect not just to a parent comment (like Reddit, Facebook, YouTube, Twitter), but to multiple pieces of content simultaneously. This is a HyperEdge.

Why do we have a like button but not an "I understand" button?

## Conversation as graph

Recording conversations live and publishing them — expanding the idea of a podcast: allow two people to have a conversation, add it to the hypergraph, and allow anyone to continue that conversation. An endless sea of conversations. Transcribe conversations between two people and add them as textual representations in the HyperGraph. Multiple translations.

## Responsibility in storytelling vs journalism

What is the difference between a story and an account of reality? Are not all accounts of reality simply stories we tell ourselves based on our perceptions? What is the responsibility of the narrator towards the reader in the crafting of a narrative?
